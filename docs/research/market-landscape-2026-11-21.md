# Own the evidence, not the agent

As of November 2026, a self-hosted, open-core "Agent Software Factory" that seals evidence for each stage from design to retirement has no direct competitor in the sources reviewed. That gap is real, but the product does not yet fill it. The repo has proven launch, safe iteration and the team-repo PR loop live. It has proven operate and retire only in dry-run, on one laptop, for one user, on small Next.js apps. The market around it is hyper-funded and consolidating fast. Enterprise AI-coding spend reached **$4.0B in 2025, up from about $0.55B in 2024** ([Menlo Ventures, Dec 2025](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-AI-in-the-enterprise/)). Cursor, Cognition, Factory.ai, Lovable, Replit and Vercel carry valuations from $5B to $60B. Buyer evidence still points to the factory's thesis. DORA 2025 finds AI raises throughput while delivery instability keeps rising. In May 2026, 60% of developers blocked agents from unapproved system changes. Gartner's November 2026 trends forecast harnesses, sandboxed preproduction, eval-driven development and a move to local infrastructure for regulated AI workloads. The defensible position is therefore not "a better coding agent." It is a **self-hosted control and evidence plane that orchestrates other vendors' agents and tools into one sealed, engine-judged record per product, from first request to decommission.** That position is not yet defensible in practice. Its moat today is a design, not an installed base. The vision and goals should be built around closing J4 and J5 live, getting one external pilot, and turning "sealed evidence" into something a third party can verify, not only something the author can show.

## Coding spend grew roughly 7x while delivery got less stable

Spending is concentrated and measured mainly at the coding layer. Menlo Ventures puts 2025 enterprise generative-AI spend at **$37B (3.2x on 2024)**. Coding is the largest departmental category at **$4.0B, 55% of departmental AI spend**, and code completion alone is about $2.3B. **Anthropic held about 54%** of the enterprise coding-model market, up from 42% six months earlier ([Menlo Ventures, Dec 2025](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-AI-in-the-enterprise/)). A wider figure, $12.8B of AI coding-tool revenue in 2026 against $5.1B in 2024, appears in The Next Web without a named estimator, so treat it as unsourced ([TNW, Apr 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/cursor-anysphere-2-billion-funding-50-billion-valuation-ai-coding)). The research found no public, dollar-denominated 2025–2030 TAM for "AI software-engineering agents" or "AI across the SDLC." Gartner's "Forecast Analysis: Agentic AI in Application Development Software, 2026" is paywalled ([Gartner doc 7941073](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/7941073)). IDC's headline forecast of **$1.3 trillion of AI spending by 2029**, with agentic AI above 26% of IT spend, includes no software-development figure ([IDC, 26 Aug 2025](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/press-releases/agentic-ai-to-dominate-it-budget-expansion-over-next-five-years-exceeding-26-of-worldwide-it-spending-and-1-3-trillion-in-2029-according-to-idc/)). Any market-size slide in the product's own materials should therefore present Menlo's measured coding spend as the anchor and label everything else as forecast.

Adoption is close to universal, but trust is not. DORA 2025 surveyed about 5,000 professionals. About **90% use AI at work**, and AI adoption is now positively associated with throughput, yet **software delivery instability continues to rise** ([InfoQ, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/09/dora-state-of-ai-in-dev-2025); [DORA 2025](https://dora.dev/dora-report-2025/)). Stack Overflow's April 2026 pulse survey (n=1,100) found agent use at work rising from **31% to 59%** and daily use from 14% to 37%. In the same survey, **60% block agents from unapproved system changes, 63% rarely or never run them on autopilot, and 68% prefer predictable single-agent setups** ([Stack Overflow, 27 May 2026](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/05/27/agents-on-a-leash-agentic-ai-remains-mostly-monitored-at-work)). Quality and security data reinforce this. GitClear measured duplicated blocks **up 81%** between 2023 and 2026 YTD, and refactored code falling from 21% to 3.8% of changed lines; its page is internally inconsistent on some units ([GitClear 2026](https://gitclear.com/the_ai_code_quality_maintainability_gap)). Veracode found that **45% of LLM code samples introduced OWASP Top-10 flaws** ([Veracode, 30 Jul 2025](https://www.veracode.com/press-release/ai-generated-code-poses-major-security-risks-in-nearly-half-of-all-development-tasks-veracode-research-reveals/)). Productivity gains are contested. METR's early-2025 RCT measured a **19% slowdown**. METR has since withdrawn confidence in its late-2025 follow-up, which showed 4–18% speed-ups, because of selection effects ([METR, 24 Feb 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)). Spotify reported candidly that its Xirp agent environment raised PRs "along with rework and wasted tokens" across more than 36,000 sessions ([Spotify, 10 Aug 2026](https://portal.spotify.com/blog/introducing-xirp)). The best-known failure is Replit's agent **deleting a production database during a code freeze in July 2025** ([Fortune, 23 Jul 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/07/23/ai-coding-tool-replit-wiped-database-called-it-a-catastrophic-failure/)).

Buyer and regulator requirements point to proportionate oversight, isolation and auditability. In November 2026 Gartner forecast that by 2028 organisations with **AI coding harness infrastructure will have 60% fewer post-deployment defects**. It also forecast that **70% of teams will adopt eval-driven development by 2028** (from under 20%), that **70% of enterprises will sandbox agentic workflows in preproduction by 2030** (from 10%), and that **60% of regulated enterprises will move AI workloads to local infrastructure by 2028** ([Gartner, 18 Nov 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-11-18-gartner-identifies-the-top-strategic-technology-trends-in-software-engineering-for-2026-and-beyond)). In May 2026 Gartner told coding-agent buyers to weigh governance, support, commercial durability and fit for regulated procurement over "product momentum alone" ([Gartner, 20 May 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-20-gartner-says-the-market-for-enterprise-ai-coding-agents-is-entering-a-new-phase-of-expansion-and-competitive-realignment)). These are forecasts, not measurements, but they read almost as a feature list for the factory. For UK buyers, the NCSC's "vibe coding spectrum" (18 Jun 2026) ties required oversight to code risk. Prototypes can be lightly governed. Code touching authentication, personal data, secrets or critical systems needs human review and blast-radius limits ([NCSC](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-07/The-vibe-coding-spectrum-approach-to-AI-assisted-software-development.pdf)). The repo's diff-based change-risk policy and its demo/pilot/production readiness tiers map directly onto that spectrum. In the EU, the AI Omnibus entered into force on 27 Jul 2026 and pushed Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 Dec 2027 ([White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/eu-ai-omnibus-enters-force-amending-ai-act)). A coding factory is unlikely to be high-risk itself; that is the research team's inference, not a legal finding. Its customers will still need traceable evidence for the regulated products they build. Exportable evidence is worth more than certification.

## The SDLC is crowded at both ends, with nothing joining design to retirement

The competitor set divides into three groups. Direct rivals compete for the same "agents run the lifecycle" buyer. Adjacent players own one or two stages and are expanding. Partners are tools the factory should drive as stations rather than rebuild. Nearly everyone covers code, test and PR. App builders deploy and host. Ops incumbents investigate incidents. **No vendor in the sources claims a governed operate-to-retire workflow, and none claims tamper-evident evidence that spans stages.** This is an absence in the sources reviewed, not proof of absence.

Three patterns follow from the table. First, "human approves the PR" is the industry's default guardrail. Approvals alone do not differentiate the factory, because Harness, Sonar, GitLab, Port and Devin all have them. Second, the operate layer is the most heavily funded and least verified segment. AWS reports **up to 75% lower MTTR and 94% root-cause accuracy** from preview customers, measured against estimated manual baselines ([AWS, 31 Mar 2026](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/mt/announcing-general-availability-of-aws-devops-agent)). The ORCA-bench figures cited in Traversal's funding story show the **best frontier agent solving only 25.3% of medium-difficulty incidents**, with up to 40% hallucination; that benchmark's independence is unverified ([TNW, 4 Nov 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/traversal-120m-series-b-1b-valuation-ai-sre)). Building a rival RCA engine would be a mistake. Third, these incumbents open themselves to orchestration. MCP is the common surface, at **97M monthly downloads under the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation** ([Futurum, 7 Apr 2026](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/mcp-dev-summit-2026-aaif-sets-a-clear-direction-with-disciplined-guardrails/)). Sonar, Grafana, AWS, Azure and Traversal all expose MCP servers or connectors. That makes "team-owned stations backed by third-party tools" technically feasible today. The same incumbents are also becoming orchestrators themselves: Snyk coordinates non-Snyk agents, AWS ingests Datadog and Grafana, and Port calls itself command-and-control. The integration surface is therefore also contested ground.

## The self-hosted, full-lifecycle quadrant is empty, and so far the factory sits there only on paper

The clearest axes the evidence supports are **deployment and governance posture** (vendor SaaS with audit logs at one end; self-hosted with engine-judged, sealed evidence at the other) against **SDLC breadth** (one stage at one end; design through retire at the other). Placement uses the coverage and deployment facts in the table above.

| | **Narrow (1–2 stages)** | **Broad (design → deploy, or more)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-hosted and evidence-governed** | OpenHands Enterprise, Cline Enterprise (code; self-hosted/VPC, RBAC, audit). Sonar agents (quality/security; self-hosted containers, BYO model) | **Agent Software Factory** (live: design → deploy → iterate → repo PR; dry-run: operate → retire). No other occupant found |
| **Vendor-hosted, audit-log governance** | Cursor, Codex, Claude Code, Antigravity, Kiro (develop/design). Datadog, AWS, Azure, Traversal, Resolve, PagerDuty (operate). Snyk Evo (secure) | GitHub Agent HQ + Agentic Workflows, GitLab DAP, Harness, Factory.ai, Devin, Port (design → review/deliver). Replit, Lovable, v0 (build → host, light governance) |

The top-right quadrant is empty because it is hard to occupy, not because nobody has noticed it. A self-hosted product spanning seven stages has to integrate with every customer's stack, survive every customer's security review, and produce evidence that auditors trust. The SaaS leaders avoid this. OpenAI's own Codex lead admitted that security, managed deployments and on-prem "still need significant work" ([Fortune, 4 Mar 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/03/04/openai-codex-growth-enterprise-ai-agents/)). Gartner's forecast that 60% of regulated enterprises will move AI workloads local by 2028 suggests the quadrant will gain buyers ([Gartner, 18 Nov 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-11-18-gartner-identifies-the-top-strategic-technology-trends-in-software-engineering-for-2026-and-beyond)). The most likely entrants are top-left players moving right (OpenHands, which calls itself "the open standard for autonomous software development") and bottom-right players offering self-managed editions (GitLab, GitHub). The second axis matters as much as the first. GitHub already has policy, audit logging and agent allow-lists ([GitHub, 28 Oct 2025](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/welcome-home-agents/)). What nobody in the sources offers is **evidence the engine decided, rather than evidence a person reviewed**: holdout scenarios stored outside every worktree, a readiness tier computed from sealed artifacts, and a decommission record that "no agent writes" (repo `docs/adr/0006-holdout-verification.md`; `docs/adr/0049-run-maintain-retire.md:64-80`). The positioning statement should claim that distinction, not "self-hosted coding agent," which OpenHands and Cline already own.

The positioning also has to carry the repo's own caveat. The 2026-10-21 critique warned that "a software factory that stops at deploy is a code generator with good paperwork" (repo `docs/planning/critique-2026-10-21.md:58-59`). Until J4 (run and maintain) and J5 (end of life) run live, the factory sits in the top-right quadrant by design and in the top-middle by proof.

## Evidence is the only moat a part-time open-core project can build

Moats in this market come from four sources. Switching costs come from being where code, pipelines and tickets live. Intangibles are brand, trust and data. Network and ecosystem effects come from integrations, marketplaces and community. Scale means capital and distribution. The rivals hold the last two in amounts the factory cannot match. Cursor reached **$2B ARR by Feb 2026** ([TNW, Apr 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/cursor-anysphere-2-billion-funding-50-billion-valuation-ai-coding)). GitHub cites **180M developers** ([GitHub, Oct 2025](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/welcome-home-agents/)). Cognition raised $1B at $26B ([Built In, 1 Jun 2026](https://builtin.com/articles/cognition-raises-1b-26b-valuation-20260601)). The only moat open to a one-person, services-led project is the second one: a body of verifiable evidence and a trust design that is hard to retrofit.

Two signals from adjacent open-core companies matter for the factory's model. Grafana, an open-core peer, raised average products per customer from **2.3 to 4.4 in two years** on the way past $600M ARR ([Grafana, 26 Aug 2026](https://grafana.com/press/2026/08/26/grafana-labs-crosses-10000-customer-milestone-as-ai-adoption-accelerates-growth-across-the-platform/)). That supports landing with one station preset ("Test + Security", say) and expanding, which is exactly the repo's composable-SDLC direction. GitLab's shift to **hybrid seat-plus-usage pricing** for agents ([GitLab, 3 Mar 2026](https://about.gitlab.com/press/releases/2026-03-03-gitlab-reports-fourth-quarter-fiscal-year-2026-financial-results/)) and credit-based pricing at Devin, Kiro and Rovo Dev show where pricing is going. Credit pricing also draws repeated complaints about budget opacity ([The Register, 18 Aug 2025](https://www.theregister.com/2025/08/18/aws_updated_kiro_pricing/); [The Register, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.theregister.com/2026/03/12/users_protest_as_google_antigravity/)). With bring-your-own-key self-hosting, the customer pays the model vendor directly, and the factory's "cost per delivered change, failures included" metric becomes a FinOps feature. One comparison needs care. The factory's observed **$3.45–$6.38 of model spend per live change** (repo `docs/planning/roadmap.md`, Oct–Nov 2026) looks favourable against a reviewer's estimate of **$33.75–$90 per Devin feature** ([dev.to, Jun 2026](https://dev.to/jovan_chan_9500711396d4e6/devin-20-review-2026-the-500-ai-engineer-is-now-20-and-bundled-with-windsurf-2h2e), secondary). The units differ: the factory's figure excludes compute and human time, and the repo itself records an unexplained ~6x gap between its cost meters. It is not a defensible public claim yet.

The services channel is where the evidence moat earns money first. Gartner predicts that **about 70% of enterprises will abandon agentic AI built by vendor forward-deployed engineers by 2028** unless they build internal capability. It cites FDE fees above **$200,000 per quarter per use case** and roughly 2,000 active FDEs against about 4x that demand ([Gartner, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-09-29-gartner-predicts-70-percent-of-enterprises-will-abandon-agentic-ai-built-by-vendor-forward-deployed-engineering-by-2028); [CIO Dive, Oct 2026](https://www.ciodive.com/news/can-forward-deployed-engineers-fdes-fix-ai-gartner/831833/)). A consultancy that leaves a self-hosted, Apache-2.0 factory and its evidence ledger inside the client ("we deploy, you own") answers that abandonment risk directly. That fits the repo's decided business order: consulting credibility first, open-core product later (repo `docs/planning/master-plan.md:220-229`). Globant's AI Pods, a subscription with token-metered capacity on its own model-agnostic platform ([Globant, 5 Jun 2025](https://www.globant.com/news/globant-introduces-ai-pods-model)), show the large-firm version of the same play.

## The whitespace is operate-to-retire evidence; the threats are GitHub, OpenHands and the name

**Whitespace** exists in four places the sources support. The first is **cross-vendor, per-stage sealed evidence**. Every incumbent keeps its own audit trail, and no source describes SLSA- or in-toto-style attestation of agent output by any competitor. The second is the **retire stage**. No vendor offers a decommission workflow, while the repo has one built (sealed `record.json` and history, ADR-0049), though not yet run live. The third is **readiness computed from evidence, mapped to risk**. This aligns with the NCSC spectrum and Gartner's eval-driven forecast, and no rival publishes a demo/pilot/production tier that the engine, not an agent, assigns. The fourth is **first-class tool gates rather than optional MCP calls**. The ai-coding-agents research found no competitor with native Snyk, SonarQube, Datadog or Grafana integrations as evidence gates. That was not specifically searched, so verify it before claiming it. Team-owned stations (RACI, a rota, a person accountable per decision) also had no equivalent in the sources, but this is the least proven part of the vision: "Nothing here is built" (repo `docs/planning/plans/stations-and-teams.md:3`).

**Threats** come from three directions. The first is **GitHub absorbing the governance plane**. Agentic Workflows went GA on 28 Oct 2026 with sandboxing, a firewall, "safe outputs" validation and a pre-apply threat-detection job ([GitHub, 28 Oct 2026](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-10-28-github-agentic-workflows-generally-available)). Agent HQ adds org-wide policy and model controls. If GitHub adds deploy evidence or a GHES edition, much of the factory's differentiation becomes a checkbox for anyone on GitHub. The factory supports only GitHub and GHE today, which makes it more exposed, not less. The second is **OpenHands or GitLab moving into the empty quadrant**. OpenHands already has the licence, air-gapped deployment, 100+ models and enterprise controls, plus far more community than the factory. GitLab has the self-managed customer base, though Duo's Self-Managed availability is not confirmed in the sources. The third is **naming collision**, which is severe. Factory.ai is a $5B company literally named "Factory," and the repo already uses its Autonomy Maturity Model for its Level 3 floor (repo `docs/practices.md:11-14`). Tessl's site banner reads "Build your software factory with Tessl Agent" (title-level only; [Tessl](https://tessl.io/blog/how-tessls-products-pioneer-spec-driven-development/)). Port's event is "PortCon: The AI Software Factory Summit" ([SiliconANGLE, 11 Dec 2025](https://siliconangle.com/2025/12/11/port-nets-100m-turn-developer-portal-agentic-ai-hub/)). The repo records Warp's "Cloud Software Factory" as an external reference (repo `docs/practices.md:174-193`), and its own master plan concedes "'Agent Factory' is crowded" (repo `docs/planning/master-plan.md:232`). "Agent Software Factory" and "ASF" cannot be differentiated in search or in a trademark filing. The open candidates (Autowright, Forgeline, Kilnworks, Throughline) avoid the collision; trademark checks are still outstanding. The product's own gaps are a fourth threat. The repo's 2026-11-15 security assessment lists **18 findings, 4 high**. Agent-written code ran outside the sandbox in builds, start checks and pods; identity headers were trusted; and a subscription token was readable in sandboxes (repo `docs/security/assessment-2026-11-15.md:5-24`). Several are fixed. MFA/SSO, signed manifests, locked image inputs and CI security gates remain open. A product whose pitch is trust cannot launch publicly with those open, and Replit's database deletion shows how one incident defines a brand.

## Vision and goals should be built on what is proven, not what is planned

The repo's vision statements have drifted: a laptop tool (README), then an org-adoptable composable SDLC (2026-10-11), then a full-lifecycle consulting demo (2026-10-21), then team-owned stations integrated with SaaS (2026-11-21). They also conflict on ownership. Principle 7 says the owner is "a named person"; the newest plan says a product is "owned by a team, never by one person" (repo `docs/planning/master-plan.md:23-27`; `docs/planning/plans/stations-and-teams.md:24-26`). The market evidence settles the order. Buyers want governed, predictable, single-agent-style control (Stack Overflow), stage-by-stage adoption (Grafana's land-and-expand, the repo's own presets) and local deployment for regulated work (Gartner). That favours **"team-owned stations integrated with existing tools, under one evidence ledger"** as the vision. "Any app, agentic first-class" becomes a later pack. The RACI reconciliation already in the plan answers the ownership conflict, and the master plan should adopt it: the team is responsible, and one person from the rota is accountable per decision.

| Claim in the repo's vision | Status (repo evidence, date) | Market relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Launch a client-worthy product to Level 3 | **Proven live** (J1, Plant watering, 2026-11-05; 10/10 requirements verified, 71/71 signals, $5.78) | Table stakes vs app builders; the evidence is the difference |
| Iterate safely: temp env, promote digest, blue/green | **Proven live** (J2, 2026-11-06; tier "pilot"). Rollback after a bad switch **not** proven | Answers DORA instability directly |
| Team-repo PR loop: assess → PR → fix CI → merge | **Proven live** (J3, 2026-11-15/16; 11 live cases; GitHub/GHE only) | Same ground as GitHub/Devin/Factory; weakest differentiation |
| Sealed, re-verifiable evidence per change | **Built and in use**; hashes, not signatures; backups now encrypted | Core whitespace; needs signing and an external verifier |
| Diff-based change risk; non-removable policy floor | **Built**, exercised in live runs | Maps to NCSC spectrum |
| Operate: watch → incident → fix → redeploy; restore drill | **Dry-run only** (J4, merged 2026-11-17) | Must be live before claiming "runs software" |
| Retire with sealed decommission record | **Dry-run only** (J5) | Unique in sources; unproven |
| Team-owned stations, rota, RACI | **Not built** (plan dated 2026-11-21) | Uncontested in sources but furthest from proof |
| Third-party tools as stations (Snyk, Sonar, Grafana) | **Not built** beyond scan providers | Partners' MCP surfaces make it feasible |
| Any model / local models | **Not built** (backlog) | Table stakes in messaging; required for the regulated-local thesis |
| Multi-user, concurrent, HA | **Not built** (single node, SQLite, 1 change) | Prerequisite for any team buyer |
| Apache-2.0 open core | **Decided, not applied** (no LICENSE in git) | Prerequisite for open-source credibility |

The goals that follow should be measurable with metrics the repo already defines in `docs/outcomes.md`: autonomy share, cost per delivered change including failures, change failure rate, lead time p50/p90, share live at Level 3, requirements verified live, and readiness tier reached. In sequence: first, close J4 and J5 live and reach the **production** tier on one product, with an alerting availability target, a passed restore drill and a sealed decommission record. Second, close the high and medium security findings that a reviewer would check first, and sign the evidence manifests, so "sealed" means verifiable by someone other than the author. Third, apply the LICENSE, rename the product, and publish a verifier a third party can run against a downloaded evidence bundle. Fourth, run one external pilot, ideally a UK regulated-sector or public-sector consulting client, using one station preset integrated with one partner tool (Sonar is the natural first, because its agents already run self-hosted with BYO models). Report baseline and after values for change failure rate and cost per change. Only then should team-owned stations, concurrency and Postgres become the next north star. Gartner's eval-driven and harness forecasts are the outside tailwind to cite. The internal discipline is the repo's own freeze rule: no new control surface until J5 has run live once (repo `docs/planning/roadmap.md:24-25`).

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. It is not whether an agent software factory can out-build Cursor, Devin or Factory.ai; it cannot and should not try. The question is whether one product can become the neutral, self-hosted place where those agents' work is judged and recorded across a product's whole life. Incumbents are converging on "human approves the PR" plus vendor-local audit logs. The scarce asset is a portable, engine-judged record linking a design decision to a test, a scan, a deploy, an incident and a retirement. That record also suits the consulting business model: it is the deliverable that stays with the client after the consultant leaves, which addresses Gartner's FDE-abandonment forecast.

The window is time-limited and the proof is incomplete. GitHub shipped most of a governance plane in October 2026. OpenHands holds the self-hosted open-source position, and "software factory" is already a contested term. The factory's credible claim today is "governed delivery and iteration, proven live." "Runs and retires software" still needs a live run, and "for teams" is still a plan. Vision and goals that tie each public claim to a sealed, externally verifiable run will turn the repo's main habit, refusing to count what has not been proven, into its market position.
